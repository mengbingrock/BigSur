## Procedure

1. Run the sample on a 0.7-0.8% agarose gel at reduced voltage. Lower percentage
   and slower running give crisp bands, which is what makes the cut clean.
   Leave an empty lane between samples, and between the ladder and the samples.
2. Move the gel to an open UV box on a glass plate and take it out of the tray.
3. Cut out the fragment with a clean sterile razor blade. Trim away excess
   agarose: less gel means less buffer and a more concentrated elution.
4. Put the slice in a labelled microfuge tube.
5. Weigh the tube, having zeroed the balance against an empty one. The weight
   stands in for volume and sets the buffer volumes in the next step.
6. Follow the spin-column kit's instructions for dissolving, binding, washing
   and eluting.
7. Measure the concentration before using the DNA for anything else.

## Notes

- Keep UV exposure short; it nicks and dimerises DNA.
- Do not image the gel on a transilluminator before cutting if you can avoid it.
